Resolution 403 · ABA House of Delegates Annual Meeting · Chicago · 2026

When recruiting outpaces the curriculum, legal education suffers.

A resolution on accelerated law-firm recruiting.
What we ask you to adopt

A resolution urging law schools, private legal employers, bar associations, and other stakeholders to:

  1. Study how accelerating recruitment timelines affect legal education, student well-being, and the entry-level hiring market; and
  2. Consider, in their own recruitment practices and policies, the educational, professional, and equitable interests of law students.
85%of 2L summer offers now made before July of the 1L year — up from 34% just one cycle earlier
18law-school student governments asked the ABA to act
Why it matters
  • For decades this happened in August, after 1L ended. The decision now lands inside the first year — some outreach begins about six weeks in.
  • Students commit before they’re ready — before first grades, before legal writing is finished, before any clinic or elective.
  • 75% tie rising anxiety directly to recruiting pressure, and a majority of surveyed Class of 2028 students say the timeline hurt them.
  • Only 25% knew these timelines before enrolling — a compressed calendar rewards students who already have networks.
  • Employers lose too — they hire on less information, and no single firm can slow down alone.

Resolution 403 is the first step out.

The students asked first. On January 1, 2026, student governments at 18 law schools (Columbia, Cornell, Duke, Georgetown, Harvard, Michigan, Northwestern, NYU, Penn, Stanford, Texas, UC Berkeley, UCLA, Chicago, Virginia, Vanderbilt, WashU, and Yale) jointly asked the ABA to review accelerating recruitment, several backed by student polls showing supermajority support. Sponsored by the Bar Association of the District of Columbia, with a coalition of law schools. Approved by the BADC Board of Directors and recommended by its Young Lawyers Section; the drafters are aware of no opposition.

Please vote to ADOPT Resolution 403.
