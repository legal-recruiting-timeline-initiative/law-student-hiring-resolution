Resolution 403 · Before the ABA House of Delegates

When recruiting outpaces the curriculum, legal education suffers.

A resolution on accelerated law-firm recruiting, before the 2026 ABA House of Delegates.

What we ask the House to adopt

A resolution urging law schools, private legal employers, bar associations, and other stakeholders to:

  1. Study how accelerating recruitment timelines affect the quality of legal education, the well-being of law students, and the long-term health of the entry-level hiring market; and
  2. Consider, in their own recruitment practices and policies, the educational, professional, and equitable interests of law students, including their need for adequate time to develop the analytical, ethical, and professional competencies that the first year of legal education is designed to foster.

The problem

Why it matters

  • For decades this happened in August, after 1L ended. The decision now lands inside the first year — some outreach begins about six weeks in.
  • Students commit before they’re ready — before their first grades, before legal writing is finished, before any clinic or elective.
  • 75% tie rising anxiety directly to recruiting pressure, and a majority of surveyed Class of 2028 students say the timeline hurt them.
  • Only 25% knew these timelines before enrolling — a compressed calendar rewards students who already have networks.
  • Employers lose too — they hire on less information about performance and interest, and no single firm can slow down alone.

Resolution 403 is the first step out: it asks the profession to study what this is doing, and to weigh students’ interests in its own recruiting.

The students asked first

A coalition of law schools

On January 1, 2026, student governments at 18 law schools (Columbia, Cornell, Duke, Georgetown, Harvard, Michigan, Northwestern, NYU, Penn, Stanford, Texas, UC Berkeley, UCLA, Chicago, Virginia, Vanderbilt, WashU, and Yale) jointly asked the ABA to review accelerating recruitment. Several backed the request with student polls showing supermajority support.

“It should give us pause that, in a period already defined by significant institutional change and disruption, one of the forces exerting the most pressure on the structure of the first-year curriculum is not pedagogical reform or accreditation standards, but employer recruiting activity.” Nikia Gray, Executive Director, National Association for Law Placement (NALP)
Please vote to adopt Resolution 403.
